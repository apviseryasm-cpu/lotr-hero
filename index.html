Samwise Gamgee is the real hero of The Lord of the Rings

Frodo carried the Ring. That is not the same thing as being the hero.

Written by Apviser. There is a live tally at the bottom, and I would genuinely like you to prove me wrong.

First, the case against me, because it deserves to be stated properly. Frodo carried the Ring for seventeen years in the Shire, and then carried it into Mordor knowing he probably would not come back. It grew heavier. It ate his memories of home. He volunteered when nobody else would, and that is not nothing.

But he didn't actually do it

At the Crack of Doom, Frodo does not throw the Ring into the fire. He claims it.

“I have come,” he said. “But I do not choose now to do what I came to do. I will not do this deed. The Ring is mine!”

The Ring is destroyed because Gollum — dancing on the edge in triumph, having just bitten off the finger — slips and falls. Not because of Frodo's will. Because of an accident, in the middle of a celebration.

The generous reading is that nobody could have done it, and Frodo got the Ring further than any being in history. I agree. But look at what that concedes. The person usually called the hero got the Ring to the door and could not open it.

The test the book actually sets

Tolkien's Ring is a machine for finding out what people are like under pressure. Gandalf will not touch it. Galadriel passes, barely. Boromir fails inside a few hundred pages.

Sam takes it. On the pass of Cirith Ungol, believing Frodo dead, he puts it on — and is offered the vision of Samwise the Strong, hero of the age, striding through a burning land with armies at his command. He puts it down again, because he wants his garden more.

He is the only person in the story who picks the Ring up and voluntarily puts it back down. Not resisted it from a distance. Not refused to touch it. Held it, was offered everything, and handed it over.

If the Ring is the test, Sam is the only one who passed it at close range. That seems to me a better definition of heroism than carrying it well until you crack.

And he had nothing to gain

Aragorn is reclaiming a throne. Gandalf is on a mission. Frodo has the world's expectation placed on him and accepts it — admirable, but a burden he was handed.

Sam goes because Frodo is going and Sam is not letting him go alone. No crown, no prophecy, no songs written about him in advance. And when Frodo's legs stop working on the last climb, Sam physically carries him up the mountain. There is no version of the story where the Ring is destroyed without Sam doing that.

"He's just the sidekick"

Tolkien's whole theme is that the great and powerful cannot fix this, and the small and unregarded do the work. Sam is that theme in a person. If the book means what it says about the value of the ordinary, then the hobbit who carries the hero up the mountain is not a supporting character. He is the argument.

So why does everyone say Frodo?

I think it depends on how you met the story. The books live inside Sam's head — his fear, his homesickness, his bafflement at how far away Frodo has gone. The films give their big moments to Frodo and Aragorn: the sacrifices, the crowning, the close-ups. If that was your version, you may be answering a slightly different question — who was the lead — and answering it correctly.
